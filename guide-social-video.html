How to promote your synthwave music with short videos

TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts are where new listeners find music. Here's what works for synth artists, including what we've learned from our own posts.

1. Hook people in the first two seconds

  • Start with a feeling, not your name. A line like "Some melodies are older than the machines that play them" makes people stay. "New single out" makes them scroll.
  • Put your strongest musical moment first. Skip the long intro: start on the drop, the lead or the vocal hook.
  • Show something worth looking at: a strong visual, a face, a synth being played, or a scene that matches the mood.

2. Make a new cut for every post

Posting the same video twice rarely works. When we reposted a release video on release day, it got about a third of the views of the first post. A new edit, a different section of the track or a fresh angle gives the second post a real chance.

3. Mix up your video styles

If every post looks the same, people stop noticing. Rotate a few formats: a mood video for the release, a studio or performance clip, a funny moment, a behind-the-scenes story. One of our best videos this month was a one-off retro game-show clip, posted in the middle of a week of similar-looking posts.

4. Bring other people in

  • Tag everyone involved: featured artists, remixers, the radio show or playlist that played you.
  • Use Instagram's collaborator option so the post also appears on their profile.
  • Ask them to comment and share. Our best "welcome" video was for an artist whose fans joined in: it got more than twice the usual views and the most comments of the week.

5. Post where your listeners are

  • TikTok usually gives the most views for a new video, and it often keeps growing for a few days. Judge a post after three days, not one.
  • Instagram Reels bring more likes, comments and shares from people who already follow you.
  • YouTube Shorts help new listeners find your full tracks and videos on the same channel.
  • Upload the same vertical video (9:16) to all three, with a custom cover, and adjust the text for each.

6. Keep the caption short and useful

One hook line, one line about the track, one link or call to action, a few genre hashtags (#synthwave, #darksynth, #retrowave). Long captions with lots of hashtags don't help a short video.

7. Learn from your numbers

Once a week, look at your top three videos. What did they share? The hook, the visual, the people tagged, the day you posted? Do more of that, and drop what doesn't work.
