How to release your synthwave music on Bandcamp and Spotify

A step-by-step guide for synthwave, darksynth and retrowave artists, from finished mix to release day.

1. Get your files ready

Before you think about dates, make sure you have everything a release needs. Missing artwork or the wrong audio format are the most common reasons a release gets delayed.

  • Audio: lossless files (WAV, AIFF or FLAC), 16 or 24-bit, one file per track. Name them in order, for example "01 Track title".
  • Artwork: square, at least 1400 × 1400 px (3000 × 3000 is best), JPG or PNG. No blur, and no web addresses or logos you don't own.
  • Words: the release title, track titles in order, a short description, and credits (who wrote, played, produced, mixed and mastered). Add lyrics if you want them shown.
  • Tags: up to 5 genre or mood tags, such as synthwave, darksynth, outrun or retrowave. Put your main genre first.

2. Pick a release date and work backwards

Choose your date first, then set deadlines before it. Streaming services need time to process a release, and you need time to promote it.

  • Streaming (Spotify, Apple Music and the rest): deliver your files at least 3 weeks before release day.
  • Bandcamp: have the release set up at least 2 weeks before, so you can take pre-orders and share the link.
  • Bandcamp editorial: if you'd like your release considered by Bandcamp's editors, plan for about 9 weeks before release day.
  • Spotify editorial playlists: once your release shows up in Spotify for Artists, you can pitch an unreleased song to Spotify's editors (one song at a time). Pitch at least 7 days before release: that also puts the song on your followers' Release Radar. The earlier you deliver, the more time you have.

Who pitches? Bandcamp editorial pitches are made by you, the artist. For Spotify, you can pitch yourself, or add your label as a team member in Spotify for Artists so it can pitch for you. Either way, put these dates in your calendar.

3. Set it up on Bandcamp

Bandcamp is where synthwave fans buy music, and where you get paid most directly.

  1. On your Bandcamp page, choose + add → album (or track) and upload your lossless audio and artwork.
  2. Set it up as a pre-order with your release date, so it goes live on the right day by itself. Don't publish it early as a normal release.
  3. Fill in the credits and description, and add your tags with your genre first.
  4. On release day, send a Bandcamp message to your followers. It goes straight to their inbox.

A note on AI: Bandcamp bans music made "wholly or in substantial part" by AI. If AI played a big part in a track, it can't go on Bandcamp.

4. Get it on Spotify and the other streaming services

You can't upload to Spotify directly. Every release reaches Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon, Deezer and the rest through a distributor: either one you sign up to yourself, or a label that distributes for you.

  • Only one distributor should deliver the same recording. If two upload it, the duplicate gets flagged and removed.
  • Once it's delivered, claim your Spotify for Artists profile so you can pitch to playlists, see your stats and update your bio and photos.

5. Promote it

Releasing is the easy part. Getting heard takes a plan for the weeks around release day.

  • Socials: post a teaser before release, then the link on the day. Short videos with your track work best.
  • Radio, playlists and blogs: synthwave has a big network of independent curators. Send your track to the ones that play your style.
  • Your fans: ask them to save, follow and share. Real listeners who save and follow send the right signals to Spotify and YouTube.
