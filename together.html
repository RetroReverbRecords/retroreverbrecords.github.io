Facts, not hype

Stronger together

No one can buy their way past the algorithms, and fake streams get removed. But a group of real listeners who all turn up for each other's releases sends exactly the signals the platforms say they reward. Here's what's true, and why it only works if everyone plays for the team.

A

What the platforms actually say

Spotify

A track earns nothing until it hits 1,000 streams a year

Since 2024, tracks need at least 1,000 streams in the previous 12 months to earn recorded-music royalties. There is also a minimum number of unique listeners, so one person playing on repeat doesn't count.

Why it matters: many real listeners beat a few loyal loops. A community of real people is exactly what gets new tracks over the line.

Spotify

Followers get your next release automatically

If a song is pitched at least 7 days before release, Spotify adds it to your followers' Release Radar.

Why it matters: every RRR member who follows every RRR artist puts each future release in front of them, every time, for free.

Spotify: pitching to playlist editors ↗
YouTube

Recommendations follow real viewer behaviour

YouTube says its recommendations use signals including clicks, watch time, survey responses, sharing, likes and dislikes.

Why it matters: members who actually watch a premiere through, like it and share it are giving the signals YouTube says it uses.

YouTube: On YouTube's recommendation system ↗
Instagram

Shares count for more than likes

Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, has named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach (people sharing a post by DM) as the key signals.

Why it matters: sending a fellow artist's reel to a friend helps it more than tapping the heart.

Bandcamp

A purchase is also an advert

When a fan buys on Bandcamp the release appears in their collection and in the feed of everyone who follows them. Bandcamp also says it features music that shows how to use Bandcamp well.

Why it matters: one member buying a €1 track puts that release in front of all their followers.

All platforms

Fake engagement backfires

Spotify's detection systems remove artificial streams, and the 1,000-stream rule was designed partly to cut off stream-farming. Bots, paid streams, muted loops and fake accounts can get releases removed.

Why it matters: only real listening helps. At RRR, faking it breaks the Code of Conduct.

B

The maths of 50 members

Say 50 members each do these five things for every RRR release in its first week.

  1. Follow the artist

    On Spotify and Bandcamp. That's 50 new followers who get every future release.

  2. Listen properly

    The whole track, not muted, not looped. 50 unique listeners on day one.

  3. Save it and add it to a playlist you play

    50 saves and 50 playlist adds: people choosing to come back to it.

  4. Share it by DM

    50 shares to people outside RRR, which is where new fans come from.

  5. Buy one if you can

    Even one €1 Bandcamp purchase shows the release in the buyer's follower feed.

Honest expectations

  • This won't make a hit on its own. It gives every release a real, early base of listeners instead of starting from zero.
  • It only works if it's every release. If people only turn up for their own, nobody gets the base.
  • The rest has to come from outside: your own fans, press, radio, playlists. The group opens the door; the music has to walk through it.

C

Why the belts matter

Most points come from supporting other artists, not from your own releases. That's on purpose: it rewards the behaviour that lifts everyone.

Your belt shows the community who turns up. Artists who support others tend to get supported back.

It's fair: small or big, everyone climbs the same way.

No shortcuts: points for fake engagement are removed.
