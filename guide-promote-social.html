How to promote your synthwave release on TikTok and Instagram

Since October 2026 we've tracked nearly 150 posts for our artists and our radio show across six networks. Here's what actually worked, and what didn't.

1. Your first line is the whole post

Most people decide in a second. Our best posts all opened with one short line that made you feel something or want to know more:

  • A mood or a mystery: "Some things were never meant to be discovered."
  • A countdown: "Two days. Then the temple wakes."
  • A number: "Nine independent artists. Two hours."
  • A funny quote: a real line from your stream or studio, in quotes.

Save the links, credits and hashtags for the end. "Out now" on its own is not a hook.

2. Pick the network for the post, not the other way round

  • TikTok gave us the most views for almost everything, as long as line one was short and strong. Funny clips did far better there than anywhere else.
  • Instagram rewards release days, show days and real people talking to camera. Clips that worked on TikTok sometimes flopped here.
  • Facebook likes release and show posts, and sometimes rewards a repost of the same video. Sales posts did poorly there.
  • Threads and Bluesky stayed small for us. Use a short version of your text (Threads has a 500-character limit).

3. Tag everyone who's in it

Our posts with every artist tagged, or with the artist added as an Instagram collaborator, beat the others again and again. When the artist shares it too, their fans see it. Ask them before you post: "Can I add you as a collaborator?"

4. Always use a custom cover

Videos often fade in from black, and the app will happily use that black frame as the cover. It ruins your profile grid. Make a 1080×1920 cover from a strong frame, add the artist and title, and keep the important part in the middle so it still reads when Instagram crops it to the grid.

5. Make a new cut, don't just repost

Posting the same video twice gave us about a third of the views the second time on TikTok. A new edit, a new first line or a different clip from the same track did much better. Plan three posts per release: an announcement, a countdown and a release-day post, each with its own hook.

6. Give it three days before you judge

Several of our videos looked like flops on day one and reached normal numbers by day three. Don't delete a post because the first hours were slow.

7. Never pay for streams

Paying for guaranteed streams, followers or playlist adds breaks the platforms' rules, and fake streams can get your music taken down. Spend the money on a good cover, an honest listen from real curators, or your next release.
