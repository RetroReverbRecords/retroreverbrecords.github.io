How to write your synthwave artist bio and press kit

Radio hosts, bloggers and labels decide in a minute whether to read on. A good bio and a tidy press kit make that minute count.

1. Write three bios, not one

  • One line (for socials and playlist pitches): who you are and your sound. "Darksynth from Turin: horror-film basslines and 80s VHS menace."
  • Short bio, 2–3 sentences (for radio intros and curator forms): add where you're from, your latest release and one thing that makes you different.
  • Long bio, 2 short paragraphs (for blogs and your Bandcamp page): your story, your influences, your releases and what's next.

Write in the third person ("Kosmic Kiss is…") so hosts can read it out on air or paste it straight in.

2. Make it sound like you

  • Lead with the feeling of your music, not your gear list. "Neon rain on an empty motorway" says more than "made in Ableton".
  • Name your scene: synthwave, darksynth, outrun, dreamwave, synthpop. It helps curators file you and helps search engines find you.
  • One real fact beats three big claims. "Played on The Bandcamp Hour" is stronger than "taking the scene by storm".
  • Keep it current. Update the bio each release. An old bio that ends in 2021 looks like a finished project.

3. Put together a simple press kit

A press kit (or EPK) is one shared folder or one web page with everything a host or blogger needs. Include:

  • Your three bios in a text file they can copy from.
  • Two or three photos or visuals, at least 1500 px wide, one landscape and one square. Faceless? A strong logo or character works too.
  • Artwork for your latest release, square, 3000 × 3000 px.
  • Your links: Bandcamp, Spotify, YouTube and your main social handles, written exactly as you want to be tagged.
  • A streaming link to the track you're pushing, plus a download for radio if they ask for one.
  • Your email, so they can reply without hunting for it.

4. Add a one-page release sheet

For each new release, add a short sheet to the folder: title, release date, track list with lengths, label, one paragraph about the release, and two or three "if you like…" artists. Hosts use this to introduce your track on air.

5. Common mistakes

  • Links that need a sign-in or a request for access. Test every link in a private browser window.
  • Huge attachments in a first email. Send a link to the folder instead.
  • Different spellings of your artist name across platforms. Pick one and use it everywhere.
  • Forgetting your handles. If they can't tag you, you lose the share.
