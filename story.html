Why I gave up my label's cut: eight years in synthwave

By Cole Bouchard (Cybertronix), founder of Retro Reverb Records.

When I started Retro Reverb Records in 2018, the dream was simple: support artists fairly.

I'd been working with Timeslave Recordings, and when I moved countries, Alex Vecchietti asked if I wanted to start something new here in Sicily. So we did. And for the whole time I've run the label, I've also been teaching full-time. RRR has never paid my bills. It's been a labour of love from day one.

Eight years later, I've just torn up the model the label was built on. Here's why.

The scene has a label problem

Synthwave is one of the most crowded genres out there, and artists are fighting for every listener. A good label should be the helping hand. Too often, it isn't. From what I've seen, a few models just don't work for artists:

  • The "release everyone" label. A very low cut, sometimes 20%, and they put out anything and everything. It sounds artist-friendly, but with dozens of releases a week, nobody gets a real push. Your release is one of a hundred.
  • The "all eggs in one basket" label. The opposite problem. Everything goes into two or three big names, and everyone else is just there for show. It's also fragile: when those acts leave, the label has nothing.
  • The merch-first label. Some did well for a while selling physical products, but artists can do print-on-demand themselves now. That edge is mostly gone.

The part that nearly finished me

I've had some genuinely bad experiences working with others in this scene. Promises were made about how things would be done, and then they weren't done that way. It set the label back about two years, and honestly, I'm still feeling the effects.

Money was never my main concern. What hurt was the trust, and the time.

I nearly gave up more than once, and I lost good people along the way. At one point someone told me, "There's no reward in this." I realised they were right. I'd been banging my head against a wall trying to get artists to support each other, and there was nothing in it for them to do so.

When I lost a close collaborator, it hit me hard. I love this label, and I wanted it to work. So I had to think differently.

Why I gave up the 35%

Let's be honest: the old 35% model never really made money. Most small labels know that feeling. You take a cut of very little, and it barely covers the work.

So I had a choice. I could keep going the same way and slowly give up on the label, or I could be brave, try something new, and stay true to why I started it in the first place.

I chose the second. The one good thing about the old model was working closely with artists, and I can still do that. What it never did was give artists real control, or reward people for showing up for each other.

So we flipped it. Artists now keep 100% of their royalties. Instead of a cut, there's a small flat membership, from €2 a month. Those micro-payments fund the label, and the artists stay in charge of their own music.

It also changes the incentive for me. A label that takes a cut can afford to coast. A label that relies on members staying can't. If we're not useful, people leave, and they should.

Will it work? I don't know yet. But I'd rather take the risk on a model that matches our values than keep running one that doesn't.

What we're doing differently

  • Artists keep 100% of their royalties, on Bandcamp and streaming.
  • We still have standards. Good production is a must, releases have to fit the genres we cover, and we follow our own release policies. But I've learnt over the years that what I think about music isn't what everyone thinks, so I try not to be the taste police.
  • Our weekly radio show, The Bandcamp Hour, is still submission-based. Being on the label doesn't guarantee a play.
  • We're building a community where supporting each other is actually rewarded, not just hoped for.

If I had to sum up what I'm proudest of, it's this: we treat artists fairly.

Where this goes

I'd love to see the scene in five years growing with new artists, new sounds and more fans, and less of the in-house bickering that has held it back.

That's the bet I'm making with RRR. Not exclusive, not a free-for-all, just fair.

Cole Bouchard (Cybertronix)
Founder of Retro Reverb Records, an independent synthwave label and artist community based in Sicily since 2018. Co-host of The Bandcamp Hour, live every Sunday on Spectrum On Air, YouTube, Facebook and XPTV3.
